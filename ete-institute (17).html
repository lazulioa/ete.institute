CASE 001 · IR-2019-11 · recomputed from public telemetry

verified event

On 16 November 2019, following fuel-price protests, Iranian authorities ordered a shutdown: cellular operators disconnected first around 14:00 local, and by 19:00 the country entered digital darkness — international connectivity down to roughly 4–5%. The near-total blackout held until 10:00 on 21 November: 111 hours. During the window, security forces killed at least 304 people; the domestic intranet stayed online. The event is cross-verified by four independent sources: IODA (BGP / active probing / telescope signals), OONI (measurements still arriving from 49 networks — proof the blackout was not total), Google traffic data, and the Amnesty International × Hertie School forensic investigation.

schematic reconstruction anchored to documented signal levels (100% → ~4–5% → recovery); not the raw series. sources: ooni.org/post/2019-iran-internet-blackout · ioda.inetintel.cc.gatech.edu · iran-shutdown.amnesty.org · pulse.internetsociety.org

CASE 002 · TO-2022-01 · single-point fragility

verified event

On 15 January 2022, the Hunga Tonga–Hunga Haʻapai eruption severed Tonga's only international subsea cable (~827 km to Fiji), cutting nearly all internet connectivity for a population of ~105,000. Mainline repair took about five weeks, restored 22 February; limited satellite links carried government traffic in between. Compiled constraint: critical connectivity requires redundant paths (ITU resilience guidance) — membership test: FAILED — one cable, no redundancy. The case shows fragility becoming a measurable governance fact without any actor violating anything.

R

Research — one question, five programmes

How can computational systems remain understandable, contestable and governable as societies shift from legal text to algorithmic infrastructure? Each programme is one decomposition of that question.

The question decomposes: (P1) whether law can be compiled without losing meaning; (P2) whether verification can run continuously and prove itself; (P3) whether the physical arenas of sovereignty can be observed neutrally; (P4) whether infrastructure models can answer for their obligations; and (P5) whether the resulting evidence can be held in common and negotiated across borders. Prototype stages, snapshot data and asserted-rather-than-computed invariants are labeled on the instrument surface itself.

P1

Computational Rule of Law

Problem: obligations written for human readers — DSA duties, AI-Act requirements, due-diligence law, co-determination data rights — are enforced against computational systems through ad-hoc, unverifiable translation. Method: compile legal frameworks into explicit constraint geometry — an obligation becomes an envelope, a threshold a half-space, a jurisdiction a polytope; compliance is a membership test. Current instruments: the Composer's policy envelope · the Schema Injection Workshop · BDI/DGB/BMWE compilation pilots.

P2

Autonomous Auditing & Evidence Systems

Problem: audit is periodic, manual and trust-based; in fast systems, violations occur between audits. Method: autonomous agents continuously test states against compiled constraints; every breach and every return to compliance emits a C2PA-shaped, hash-signed record into an open registry; grounded in Ruffini & Castaldo's algorithmic-persistence theory (external, CC-BY). Current instruments: the trust engine · the live forensic loop · the Spatial AI Agent roadmap.

P3

Orbital, Spectrum & Network Governance

Problem: the arenas where sovereignty is now negotiated — orbital slots, spectrum, low-altitude airspace, subsea cables, routing — generate physical telemetry, but no neutral instrument turns it into treaty-grade evidence. Method: a seven-layer open-data architecture (OpenSky · Electrosense · OONI/IODA · RIPE · EU DSA database · Copernicus · cable registries) fused into one auditable model. Current instruments: the Landscape Instrument v8 (live OONI/GDELT) · the EO slope · the registry · Day 2 live forensic queries.

P4

Governable Digital Twins

Problem: digital twins represent infrastructure but do not answer for it; a model that cannot carry its own legal constraints is a rendering, not a governance object. Method: twins whose geometry embeds the constraint set of the jurisdictions they operate in — intelligent twins that self-audit supply chains, energy allocations and cross-border logistics. Current work: the Intelligent Twin lab · the Composer import→audit workflow · OUIM — the Ground·Sky·Space design platform (applied venture) · the neutral-node logistics protocol.

P5

Evidence Commons & Science Diplomacy

Problem: evidence infrastructures are either state-controlled or commercially captured; civil society lacks a neutral permanent registry, and standard-setting lacks a venue grounded in verifiable data rather than declarations. Method: an open forensic registry (free audit queries for journalists and human-rights NGOs), an annual convening where participation requires technical embedding (the Terra Forum), and a peer-reviewed standards publication delivered to legislatures (the Sovereign Spatial Bluebook). The Aesthetics of Evidence Lab sits here — legibility research, because shared geometry localizes disagreement.

A

About — an independent institute for computational governance

We do not publish opinions about auditability — we publish instruments, and the signed records they produce.

ÉTÈ Institute (Eternal Terra Ear · 永地耳) is an independent research institute for computational governance, fiscally hosted by Open Source Europe in Brussels, working bilingually in English and Chinese across Brussels, Berlin and Beijing. We hold one thesis: as governance migrates from legal text to algorithmic infrastructure, it remains legitimate only if verification is constructive.

Commitment 01

Constructive verification

Every claim we make about a system is backed by a model a third party can run.

Commitment 02

Evidence over assertion

Outputs are cryptographically signed, hash-anchored and archived; methodology published in the open. Our results do not require trusting us.

Commitment 03

Contestability by design

The communities monitored by a model must be able to inspect, recompute and challenge it — a design requirement, not an ethics annex.

INDEPENDENCE & FUNDING — three monies, strictly separated. Sponsorship buys technical embedding (ingestion slots, schema compilation) — never editorial control. Grants fund the commons: the open registry, free audit queries for journalists and human-rights NGOs, bursaries. Investment enters only the service entity — never the protocol or registry, which are held for the commons in perpetuity. The registry is not for sale; that sentence is our governance model. Dual-use here means investigative and commercial — never military.

Theoretical ground · Ruffini & Castaldo, Pattern, Persist! (2026)

Algorithmic persistence: a compressed identity that survives the filter of time

Our agents follow the algorithmic agent's triad — a Modeling Engine, a scalar Objective Function, a Planning Engine: model-mediated regulation. Every C2PA manifest is exactly that "compressed identity"; every boundary check is an irreversible act of regulation across a thin, thermodynamically costly boundary (its Landauer cost is the price of trust). By the Algorithmic Regulator Theorem, a regulator shares mutual algorithmic information with what it regulates — an auditor must contain a model of the audited. This is why the evidence must persist with the instrument. (external reference: Ruffini & Castaldo, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.21278422, CC-BY 4.0 — not an ÉTÈ publication)

Step one is complete: geometric semantic modelling. Next: giving this geometric world the spatial intelligence to reason and to resist forgery. The technical architecture whitepaper launches on Day 3 of the Terra Forum.
